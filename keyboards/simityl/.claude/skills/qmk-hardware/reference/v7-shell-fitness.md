# Top55mmTrackball v7 — shell fitness review

Source: two Fusion 360 screenshots of `Top55mmTrackball v7`, captured 2026-08-05.

- `top55mm-trackball-v7-top.png` — TOP view, looking into the socket, ball hidden
- `top55mm-trackball-v7-bottom.png` — BOTTOM/BACK view, socket exterior with both mounts

Read alongside `../SKILL.md` § "Dual-sensor yaw scroll". Note the skill still describes
`v2` as the reference document; v7 supersedes it.

## What the screenshots show

Two sensor apertures, bored through the socket wall with raised collars:

- **Sensor A** — low, near the bottom pole, displaced toward the **front** (user side).
- **Sensor B** — high on the **back** side, on a bracket that stands proud of the sphere's
  silhouette in the bottom view. Its aperture reads as an ellipse only because it is
  foreshortened at a grazing angle — consistent with a steeply-inclined, near-equatorial
  mount, not with a slotted hole. Confirm it is a true circle.

**This is the asymmetric pole + equator layout**, the second family in the skill, with the
equatorial sensor on the far side and hand clearance kept on the near side — exactly the
"cut the near side, leave the far side full height" recommendation. The structural choice
is right.

## Verdict

**Fit for purpose as drawn.** cond 1.48 against a 1.41 floor, yaw noise gain ~0.99, CPI
leakage under 1 %. Nowhere near the conventional-cup failure mode.

Three conclusions worth acting on:

1. **θ_B is nearly a free variable between 70° and 90°.** Pushing B from 70° to 80° buys
   1.66 % → 0.84 % on the CPI term while the rotation term sits at ~5.1 % either way and
   barely moves. Rotation error dominates at every geometry in this family, so **take the
   mechanically easiest θ_B** — hand clearance, ball removal and printability outrank the
   last few degrees. Only below ~60° does the geometry start costing real performance.

2. **A's offset direction matters far more than its magnitude.** Front-displaced (as it
   reads) opens γ to 95° and is fine — better than a true pole mount. Back-displaced puts
   both sensors on one meridian, collapsing γ to 65° and cond to 1.86. Verify the sign.

3. **~5 % rotation leakage is inherent to this layout and cannot be designed out of the
   shell.** Scroll rides on B's u-axis alone, with no partner to cancel against. The
   per-sensor ψ calibration in the skill is mandatory for this build, not optional.

## Checks to run in Fusion before printing

Measure, don't infer — all of these are cheap in the model and expensive after a print.

- [ ] Angle from the socket axis to each aperture axis → θ_A, θ_B. Use the ball body's
      centre as the vertex; both aperture axes should pass through it.
- [ ] Azimuth of A relative to B. Confirm A is displaced toward the **front**, not the back.
- [ ] Standoff at **both** pockets: 2.96 mm PCB-face-to-ball-surface, normal to the surface.
      This is the proven value from the working build — do not "correct" it toward 2.4 mm.
- [ ] Each aperture bored along the local **normal** (through ball centre), not vertically.
      An obliquely-bored high aperture defocuses across the imaging window and skews the
      sensor's axes in a way the fusion matrix will not model.
- [ ] B's aperture is a circle, not a slot. A slot leaves ψ under-constrained on the one
      sensor whose rotation error feeds scroll directly.
- [ ] **All three bearings still present**, spread ~120°, all below the equator. Confirm B's
      bracket did not displace one — two-point support will let the ball rock, and rocking
      is read as yaw.
- [ ] Ball removal path: B's mount stands above the equator on the back side. Check the ball
      still clears for cleaning (tilt-out, or the shell splits).
- [ ] Hand clearance on the near/front side at the rim height actually drawn.
- [ ] Cable routing for B, and light/lint ingress at the high aperture — it is far more
      exposed than a pole mount.

## Firmware follow-ups this geometry implies

- Second CS pin: **GP12, GP13 or GP14** (free, broken out, no alternate function).
- `POINTING_DEVICE_DRIVER = custom`; stock wrappers only ever touch sensor 0.
- Leave `ROTATIONAL_TRANSFORM_ANGLE` at 0 — it is global, so it cannot express two
  different mounting angles. Fold both rotations into the fusion matrix.
- Both sensors must be the same part; the PMW3389 pair on hand is the build target.
- Regenerate the Q16 constants from the **measured** θ/α rather than interpolating the
  θ=80/α=±45 set in the skill — those are for the symmetric layout, not this one.
